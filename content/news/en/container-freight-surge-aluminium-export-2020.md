Between June and December 2020 the Shanghai Containerized Freight Index roughly tripled. Spot rates for a 40ft container from Shanghai to North Europe moved from a few hundred dollars to more than **USD 4,000**, and equipment shortages meant the rate was sometimes not the constraint — the box itself was. For aluminium products, which sell at a few thousand dollars per tonne, freight went from a rounding error to a line that could decide whether an order was profitable.

## What happened

- **Blank sailings met a demand snap-back.** Carriers cancelled services in the second quarter of 2020 and then could not restore them fast enough when orders returned in the third.
- **Containers went the wrong way.** Empty equipment piled up in Europe and North America instead of returning to Asia, so exporters faced booking windows of three to four weeks.
- **Rates became volatile within a single negotiation.** A quote valid for 30 days could be under water in 10.

## Why aluminium felt it more than most

Aluminium sheet, coil and foil are **low value per cubic metre and high volume per tonne**. A 20-tonne coil of 1050 sheet at around USD 2,400/t is worth roughly USD 48,000; the same box, filled with consumer electronics, can carry several times that value. So the same freight increase is a 2% add-on for electronics and a 6–9% add-on for aluminium coil.

That arithmetic changed buyer behaviour in three visible ways:

1. **Order size went up.** Buyers consolidated from 5-tonne to 20-tonne lots to amortise the fixed cost of a container.
2. **FOB replaced CIF.** More buyers took freight risk themselves, because their own forwarder could book cheaper than a mill quoting CIF at spot.
3. **Delivery terms got renegotiated, not the price.** Sellers who insisted on CIF lost orders to sellers who offered FOB and let the buyer handle the box.

## What it means for buyers

- **Ask for the freight separately.** A single delivered number hides which part is metal, which is conversion, and which is logistics. Once separated, you can see which line is actually moving.
- **Build a freight clause into the contract.** An index-linked cap, or a share of any increase above a threshold, is standard now and was almost unknown in 2019.
- **Check the port, not just the mill.** In 2020–2021 the bottleneck was often the departure port's equipment availability, not the mill's production schedule.
- **Do not use a container as a warehouse.** Buyers who ordered early to beat rate increases then paid storage at destination.

## Where it points

The 2020 freight shock permanently changed how Chinese aluminium is exported: **FOB became the default**, freight surcharges became explicit, and quote validity shortened. Those three changes survived the rate normalisation that followed in 2022–2023 and are still in force.
