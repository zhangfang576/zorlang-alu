On 1 October 2023 the EU's Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism entered its transitional period. For importers of aluminium into the EU, this created a quarterly reporting obligation covering **embedded emissions** — with no financial payment until the definitive period began in 2026. It was a data exercise, and it was the most consequential data exercise the aluminium industry had been asked to perform.

## What had to be reported

- **Goods and quantities**, by CN code, for each installation of origin.
- **Direct emissions** from producing the metal — for aluminium, essentially the electricity consumed in electrolysis plus on-site fuel.
- **Indirect emissions** from purchased electricity, reported separately.
- **Actual values preferred; default values permitted at first.** Default values were deliberately conservative, which meant that using them was cheap in the transitional period and expensive later.

## Why "just use default values" was a trap

The transitional period allowed importers to report default values for a limited time. Many did. The problem is structural: a default value is set high enough to be safe for the regulator, so a producer with genuinely low-carbon metal — hydro or recycled — was assigned an average that erased its advantage.

Once the definitive period arrived, the supplier that could document **actual** emissions paid less than the one that could not. The gap between the two was often larger than the commercial difference between competing mills.

## What it means for buyers and suppliers

- **Embedded emissions are now a product attribute.** Alongside temper and tolerance, a coil has a carbon number, and that number has a price.
- **Recycled content is the cheapest carbon reduction.** Secondary aluminium carries roughly 5% or less of the direct emissions of primary. Where the standard permits, specifying recycled content is the fastest route to a low CBAM bill.
- **Data quality is a sales asset.** A mill that can produce installation-level electricity and fuel data, verified, wins over one that can only offer a country average.
- **The boundary is direct plus indirect.** Do not confuse CBAM's scope with a full life-cycle assessment (LCA) footprint. They answer different questions and are not interchangeable in customer conversations.

## Where it points

The transitional period ran for two years. On 1 January 2026 the mechanism moved to a payment regime, and the EU has proposed extending it to a long list of downstream steel- and aluminium-intensive products from 2028. The companies that treated 2023 as a paperwork exercise spent 2026 buying carbon certificates they could have avoided.
