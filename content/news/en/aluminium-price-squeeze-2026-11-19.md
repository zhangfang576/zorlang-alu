Over the past 10 days the aluminium market has run through a rare sequence: a squeeze on the overseas contract, then a catch-up fall on the domestic one. LME cash ran up to USD 3,332/t before falling nearly 10% in a single session the next day; SHFE aluminium's most-active contract slipped to RMB 21,840/t, a low since the end of last year. For companies exporting aluminium products, this is no longer a simple question of direction but of **managing the domestic–overseas spread** and **managing quote validity**.

**Conclusion first**: what fell was the premium, not the aluminium. Over the next 7–10 days SHFE aluminium is likely to trade in a RMB 21,500–22,800/t range and LME 3M in USD 2,950–3,150/t; the real risk comes not from the trend but from volatility.

## 1. Price recap: two worlds in 10 days

The core of this move is not supply and demand but **a squeeze created by highly concentrated stocks**. On 12 November, Cai Lian Press reported that the LME aluminium market faced its most severe squeeze in decades — stocks falling sharply, with two entities holding close to 80% of the remaining available tonnage. Over the following four trading sessions, cash and 3M prices decoupled completely.

Put these data together and the conclusion is clear: **what fell was the premium, not the aluminium**. LME stocks fell from 509 kt in early January to around 220 kt in mid-November, a decline of about 57%; available tonnage is extremely concentrated, so tightness in any single prompt date is amplified into a price spike — and once the squeezing party closes out, the retracement is just as violent.

## 2. Fundamentals: four threads decide how far the price can fall

### 1. Supply: the ceiling is soft, and output is already above it

National Bureau of Statistics data show China's electrolytic aluminium output in October 2026 at **3.85 Mt, +1.8% year on year**. Operating capacity held at a high of 44,xx0 kt, with utilisation close to full. The 45 Mt policy ceiling constrains **compliant new capacity**, not actual output — since March this year national output has been running above the ceiling (Antaike basis), and at high margins existing capacity can still release 1%–5% of elasticity by raising current intensity.

On seasonal disruption, Yunnan has entered the dry season; lower hydropower output is a routine variable in every fourth quarter. As of publication there is no large-scale curtailment announcement, but this is the supply-side variable most worth tracking over the next two months.

### 2. Costs: the floor is sinking, so a lower price is not a loss

- Alumina futures most-active closed at **RMB 2,614/t** on 16 November; the LME alumina quotation was about **USD 398/t**.
- Antaike estimates the weighted average full cost (tax included) of electrolytic aluminium in January 2026 at **RMB 16,100/t**, down RMB 3,483 or 17.8% year on year, with alumina, anode and power costs all moving lower together.
- Alumina is expected to be in surplus by about **3.7 Mt** for the full year; Indonesia adds 3 Mt of capacity in 2026, taking total capacity above 13 Mt/yr, and has already moved from net importer to net exporter.

This means that even with aluminium back at RMB 22,000/t, the industry still has close to RMB 6,000/t of margin. **The cost line is no longer hard support; smelters have little incentive to cut, and supply rigidity is stronger rather than weaker**.

### 3. Demand: domestic demand is negative, exports are the only increment

In the first half domestic electrolytic aluminium consumption rose only 0.5% year on year, but excluding extruded product exports, **real domestic demand was -0.6% year on year** (Mysteel basis). The small rise in apparent consumption came entirely from exports.

Customs data: September exports of unwrought aluminium and aluminium products were **656 kt** (+4.8% month on month, +25.9% year on year); October fell back to **594 kt** (-9.4% month on month), with January–October cumulative at 5,914 kt, +17.8% year on year. **The October month-on-month fall is the first deceleration signal in this year's strong export run**.

### 4. Inventories: the double-low structure is unchanged

LME stocks at the 220 kt level, domestic aluminium ingot social inventory 501 kt (3 November), aluminium billet inventory 131 kt; weekly billet deliveries fell back to the 40 kt level in 9–15 November. **Low stocks plus high concentration means the ground for a squeeze is still there**, and volatility will not be low.

## 3. Policy and customs: five variables that must be watched

| Variable | Latest status | Impact on aluminium product exports |
|---|---|---|
| Export tax rebate | The rebate on aluminium product exports was cancelled on 1 December 2024; in the 2026 tariff schedule the provisional rate is 0% for unwrought unalloyed aluminium and 15% for unwrought aluminium alloy | Aluminium products compete on a tax-inclusive price, so the price advantage narrows |
| EU CBAM | Enters its definitive phase on 1 January 2026; certificates go on sale from 1 February 2027, first surrender deadline 30 September 2027. Aluminium counts **direct emissions** only; green power gets no relief. Q3 2026 certificate price €82.32/tCO₂e | Chinese primary aluminium on the default-value route is about €157/t; recycled aluminium (0.05–0.1 tCO₂/t) has a clear advantage |
| US 232 | 50% baseline rate on aluminium and derivatives (from June 2025); some derivatives cut to 15% from 8 June 2026; in August a consultation opened on 14 additional product categories (25%) | Direct exports to the US are effectively closed; transhipment and third-country assembly demand rises |
| Anti-dumping | On 17 November, Argentina issued a negative final determination on Chinese aluminium sheet and will not continue collecting duties (positive); the EU maintains long-standing anti-dumping duties on Chinese aluminium extrusions and foil | The Latin American window opens; EU business has to be recalculated on post-duty cost |
| Bauxite policy | On 17 November, Guinea proposed a bauxite price index and is considering export restrictions; shipments to China from 1 January to 13 November were 155 Mt, +19.08% year on year; CIF about USD 71.3/t | If a quota is implemented, alumina costs rise and aluminium prices are supported in reverse |

Also worth noting: northern China entered the heating season on 15 November; emergency emission cuts during heavy pollution episodes are a periodic disturbance to alumina calcination and prebaked anode operations — a supply shock that is "short, fast and reversible".

**Five indicators to check every day**

1. **LME stocks and the cancelled-warrant share**: a break below 210 kt with cancelled warrants rising, and the squeeze may flare up a second time
2. **Turning point in domestic ingot social inventory**: 500 kt is the psychological line; the start of restocking means the domestic market catches down
3. **Alumina at RMB 2,600/t**: a break below and costs keep collapsing, opening room below aluminium prices
4. **Guinea policy documents**: the key jump is from "proposed index" to "formal quota"
5. **Dollar index**: has repeatedly hit stage highs recently and is the macro variable weighing on the metals complex

## 5. Four practical suggestions for export quotes

1. **Cut quote validity from 15 days to 3–5 days.** This week LME cash moved more than 15% across four trading sessions; a fixed-price quote valid for more than 10 days means carrying the volatility on the customer's behalf.
2. **Add an aluminium price linkage clause to contracts.** Use the average settlement of SHFE most-active (or LME 3M) over the five trading days before shipment as the basis; where it moves beyond ±3% of the agreed range, adjust pro rata and keep the conversion fee fixed.
3. **Lock long orders with futures.** Any fixed-price order longer than 3 months should be sold hedged on SHFE at the same time; those with LME accounts can build a range with options.
4. **Break CBAM out as a separate line item in European quotes.** On the default-value route, Chinese primary aluminium is about €157/t and aluminium products and finished goods average about €340/t (industry estimate). At the same time, offer recycled aluminium grades to EU customers as the "low-carbon option" — currently the only route that actually saves money under CBAM.
