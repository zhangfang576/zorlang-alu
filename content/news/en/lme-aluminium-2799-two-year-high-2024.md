In May 2024 LME aluminium reached about **USD 2,799/t**, its highest since June 2022, before fading. The driver was one step upstream of the metal: **alumina**.

## What happened

- **Alumina supply tightened.** Bauxite availability from Guinea was disrupted by logistics and by a dispute over a major mining project, and Chinese alumina refineries ran close to capacity. Alumina prices rose sharply, lifting the cost floor for every smelter without integrated supply.
- **Chinese output was capped.** With operating capacity near the 45 Mt ceiling and Yunnan recovering from a dry season, there was no elastic supply to answer higher prices.
- **Financial positioning amplified the move.** Managed-money length in aluminium rose to multi-year highs, and the rally accelerated once technical levels broke.
- **The physical market did not follow.** Spot premiums in Asia were flat to lower, and Chinese downstream demand — construction profiles, PV frames — was slowing.

## Why the peak did not hold

By early June the price had given back most of the gain. Two reasons:

1. **Alumina normalised.** Once bauxite shipments resumed and refineries returned, the cost-push argument weakened.
2. **Demand elasticity showed up.** At USD 2,800/t, Chinese fabricators deferred purchases and ran down inventories. The physical market simply stopped buying at the paper price.

## What it means for buyers

- **Cost-push rallies need a demand floor to persist.** A price driven by feedstock with weak end-demand is a trade, not a regime. The right response is to shorten quote validity, not to lock annual tonnage.
- **Alumina is the leading indicator.** For aluminium, watch alumina and bauxite before watching aluminium. The 2024 move, and the 2026 Guinea story, both started upstream.
- **Regional premiums are the reality check.** When the LME rises and the premium falls, the physical market is telling you the rally is financial.
- **Beware of buying at two-year highs on a supply story.** In May 2024 buyers who locked Q3 at spot paid roughly 10% above the July price.

## Where it points

May 2024 is the cleanest recent example of an **upstream cost shock that ran out of demand**. It also established alumina and bauxite as the variables Chinese aluminium buyers watch first — a habit that paid off in 2026, when Guinea's export policy became the dominant headline.
