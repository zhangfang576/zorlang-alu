China's advantage in aluminium is no longer price — it is the system behind the price: roughly 60% of global primary aluminium output, processing capability across all six product forms, and a low-carbon supply chain that can now be documented. For buyers, that means the hidden cost of switching suppliers usually exceeds a few dozen dollars per tonne — provided duty, tax and carbon costs are counted in the same ledger.

**Bottom line:** China's edge is a system advantage, not a price advantage. A system advantage survives tariffs; a price advantage does not.

## 1. Scale: about 60% of global primary aluminium, and the only complete chain

China produces roughly **60%** of the world's primary aluminium and is the only country with a complete chain from imported bauxite through alumina, smelting, processing and recycling. National Bureau of Statistics data show October 2026 output at **3.85 million tonnes, up 1.8% year on year**, running near full capacity under the 45 million tonne policy ceiling.

Scale is not only about volume. When a buyer needs a spot top-up, a specification change or a split shipment, only a deep capacity pool can absorb it — a point proven during the 2021–2022 European energy crisis, when smelter curtailments pushed global buyers toward Chinese supply.

## 2. Range: from 1050 to 8011, six forms from one supplier

A single purchase order covering 6061 plate, 5052 coil, 6063 extrusion and 1050 foil is normally one supplier in China — and four mills, four quotations and four sets of standards elsewhere.

The range spans the 1xxx to 8xxx series and all six forms: plate, bar, tube, coil, foil and extrusion, in tempers from O through T6 and T651. Marine and chemical applications can start from [5052 plate](/products/5052/), structural and tooling work from [6061 plate](/products/6061/), and architectural or industrial profiles from [6063 extrusion](/products/6063/).

> This is not "cheaper at any single point" — it is a lower total cost of combination: one enquiry, one document set, one delivery date.

## 3. Speed: clusters compress the sample-to-production cycle

The main processing clusters — Wuxi, Foshan, Linyi, Gongyi — concentrate casting, hot and cold rolling, extrusion, anodising, finishing, die making and consumables within roughly an hour's drive. A new die, a trial extrusion and an anodised sample are typically measured in days, not weeks.

For buyers this translates directly into **shorter development cycles** and **lower trial-and-error cost**, especially for custom profiles and special tempers.

## 4. Cost: not the world's lowest, but the most stable

Antaike puts China's weighted average full cost of electrolytic aluminium at about **RMB 16,100 per tonne** in January 2026, down 17.8% year on year, with alumina, anode and power costs all easing. A high share of captive power, large cell capacity and localised consumables keep the cost curve comparatively flat.

Stability matters more than the level: Europe is constrained by energy prices and the Middle East by geopolitics and shipping, while China's power and consumable supply has seen no supply-cut shock in three years. For buyers signing annual frame agreements, stability is worth more than the lowest quote.

Exports remain the one certain growth engine: 5.914 million tonnes over January–October, up 17.8%. But October fell **9.4%** month on month — the first deceleration in an otherwise strong year, and one that could reflect either the end of restocking or pull-forward ahead of tariffs.

## 5. Low carbon: recycling and carbon data are becoming the new currency

The Aluminium Industry High-Quality Development Plan (2025–2027) targets more than 15 million tonnes of secondary aluminium output and raises the renewable-power share for smelters from 25% in 2025 to 30% by 2027 — a 2030 target pulled forward by three years.

With the EU CBAM now in force, this becomes concrete: aluminium is assessed on direct emissions only, and secondary aluminium emits roughly 0.05–0.1 t CO₂ per tonne, so an actual-value route brings the CBAM obligation close to zero. Chinese suppliers can now offer that low-carbon option, not merely promise it.

## 6. The constraints that must be priced in

- **The price edge is narrowing.** The export tax rebate on aluminium products was withdrawn in December 2024; exports now compete on tax-inclusive prices.
- **Markets are fragmenting.** US Section 232 duties remain at 50%, the EU keeps anti-dumping measures on extrusions and foil, and CBAM adds compliance cost; Latin America, the Middle East and Southeast Asia are the new growth markets.
- **High-end grades still lag.** Aerospace plate, automotive outer panel and some high-end electronic foil still trail on batch consistency and qualification coverage — confirm by application, not by grade code alone.

## 7. Conclusion: compare systems, not unit prices

A practical test for buyers: score candidates on capacity, form coverage, delivery reliability, mill certificates and carbon data in one table — then look at the price per tonne. Purchasing on unit price alone usually pays the difference back through lead times, specification changes or compliance gaps.

> The real moat is being able to supply six product forms on one phone call and ship on time. Tariffs do not remove that capability, but the wrong supplier does.
