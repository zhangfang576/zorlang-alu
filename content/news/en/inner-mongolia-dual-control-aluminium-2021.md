In March 2021, smelters in Inner Mongolia were ordered to reduce electricity consumption to meet regional energy-intensity targets. Baotou and Tongliao — two of China's denser aluminium clusters — saw load reductions and, in some cases, the shutdown of older pots. For the first time in the cycle, the aluminium price started moving on **electricity rather than on orders**.

## What happened

- **The mechanism was administrative, not economic.** Smelters were not responding to a high power price; they were responding to a regional cap on total energy consumption.
- **The cut was small, the signal was large.** The affected tonnage was a fraction of Chinese output, but it demonstrated that the 4,500 Mt capacity ceiling was going to be enforced through energy policy rather than through permits alone.
- **The market re-rated Chinese supply.** LME aluminium had spent late 2020 around USD 2,000/t; through the first half of 2021 it moved up steadily as the market priced in slower Chinese supply growth.

## Why it mattered beyond Inner Mongolia

China's aluminium smelting is concentrated in provinces where coal-based power is cheap: Xinjiang, Inner Mongolia, Shandong, Henan. Those are also provinces with the heaviest energy-intensity pressure. The 2021 policy made a structural point: **the cheapest power is no longer the cheapest place to smelt**, because the energy quota has a cost even when the tariff does not.

That re-rating is what pushed new capacity toward Yunnan's hydropower — and set up the second shock of 2021, when the rains did not come.

## What it means for buyers

- **Watch provincial policy, not just national output.** A regional notice can move the price before any national statistic shows it.
- **Expect conversion cost to follow electricity.** When smelting is constrained by power, the same logic reaches rolling and extrusion: energy is a larger share of conversion cost than most buyers assume, typically 8–15% for sheet.
- **Do not read a small cut as a small effect.** In a market running at 96% utilisation, removing one or two percent of supply is enough to invert the price curve.
- **Check the smelter's province when qualifying a supplier.** The same company can have assets with very different energy exposure.

## Where it points

The March 2021 notice was the opening move in a six-month rally that took LME aluminium from about USD 2,000/t to above USD 3,200/t by October. It also established the pattern that has repeated since: **Chinese supply is now managed by policy, and policy is seasonal and provincial.**
