On 12 April 2024 the United States and the United Kingdom announced coordinated action barring the LME and CME from accepting Russian-origin aluminium, copper and nickel produced on or after 13 April 2024. The LME confirmed it would no longer warrant new Russian aluminium. LME aluminium rose sharply, reaching around **USD 2,720/t** in the following sessions.

## What the measure actually did

It did not remove Russian metal from the world. Russia still produced and sold roughly 4 Mt a year, largely to Asia. What the measure removed was Russian metal's **deliverability into the Western futures contract**.

That distinction is the whole story:

- **Total supply was unchanged.** No smelter closed because of the announcement.
- **Deliverable supply to the LME shrank.** The pool of metal that could settle a futures contract became smaller and more expensive to assemble.
- **The spread did the pricing.** Rather than a large move in the absolute price, the market repriced the cost of deliverability — visible in the cash–3M structure and in regional premiums.

## Why this matters for physical buyers

A futures contract is a promise of delivery. When the deliverable pool narrows, three things follow that reach the physical market:

1. **Warrant premiums rise.** Metal already on warrant becomes more valuable than identical metal off-warrant.
2. **Regional premiums diverge further.** Europe and the US pay for deliverability; Asia pays less.
3. **Basis risk increases.** A mill quoting "LME plus premium" has to decide which premium, for which location, with what delivery window.

## What it means for buyers

- **Separate price risk from deliverability risk.** Hedging the LME does not hedge the premium, and since 2024 the premium is the larger variable in Europe and North America.
- **Ask what the warrant covers.** For buyers using LME-referenced contracts, confirm that the reference price is buildable from metal you are actually allowed to receive.
- **Expect origin questions on delivery.** Even where metal was produced before the cut-off, documentation proving the production date is now part of delivery.
- **Watch the cash–3M spread as an early signal.** Backwardation in aluminium historically signals physical tightness faster than the outright price does.

## Where it points

April 2024 turned "deliverability" into a priced attribute of aluminium. Combined with the 200% US tariff of 2023 and CBAM from 2026, the metal now carries three separate policy layers — origin, duty and carbon — on top of its cost curve.
