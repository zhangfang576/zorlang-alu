By the end of 2025, China's built electrolytic aluminium capacity had reached about **44.83 Mt** against a policy ceiling of **45 Mt**, with operating rates near 96% and full-year output of roughly **44 Mt**. In the same year, the World Bureau of Metal Statistics estimated a global primary aluminium deficit of **0.8–1.3 Mt** for 2025 — with a shortfall of 1.29 Mt already recorded in the first nine months.

## The arithmetic that now governs the market

For two decades, Chinese supply was the world's shock absorber: prices rose, capacity was built, prices fell. That mechanism is now closed.

- **The ceiling is administrative.** New capacity requires replacing existing capacity, so the national total cannot grow.
- **Utilisation has no headroom.** At 96% operating rates, there is no idle capacity to restart into a shortage.
- **Demand keeps growing in specific segments.** Photovoltaics, grid investment, EV structures and packaging have offset weakness in construction.
- **The gap is filled by imports or by price.** With Chinese output capped, a global deficit is resolved either by higher prices or by new supply outside China — which is slower, more expensive, and often higher-carbon.

That is the structural case for a higher price floor, and it is why 2026 opened with LME aluminium near USD 3,000/t rather than in the USD 2,400s.

## What it means for buyers

- **Assume no supply elasticity from China.** If you are planning annual tonnage, plan against a capped system. Do not assume you can buy your way out of a shortage with a higher offer; the metal may not exist.
- **Lock volume, not just price.** In a capped market, availability risk exceeds price risk. A price-only hedge does not secure tonnage.
- **Expect conversion capacity to consolidate.** With metal constrained, the competitive advantage moves to mills with stable metal allocation and certification, not to the cheapest reroller.
- **Watch the export number.** Chinese exports are now the marginal balancing item for the world. When exports fall, the ex-China market tightens first.

## Where it points

The 45 Mt ceiling converted aluminium from a cyclical commodity into a structurally constrained one, at least within China. The consequence shows up in the 2026 price structure: a higher floor, sharper squeezes, and a premium for anyone who can document low-carbon production.
