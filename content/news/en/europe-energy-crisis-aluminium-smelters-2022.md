By the autumn of 2022, European primary aluminium producers had curtailed roughly **half of the region's smelting capacity**, and about a third of it had closed for good. The cause was not aluminium demand — it was natural gas at prices that made electrolysis a loss-making activity at any plausible metal price.

## What happened

- **Power contracts rolled over at ten times the old level.** Smelters that had bought forward were protected for a while; when those contracts expired, the economics broke.
- **Restarts became uneconomic, not just expensive.** Restarting a potline costs capital and takes months; doing it into a market with uncertain gas prices was a bet most producers declined.
- **The region became structurally short.** Europe now imports a larger share of the aluminium it consumes than at any time in the previous two decades.
- **The premium did the work.** With LME prices falling in the second half of 2022, the Rotterdam duty-paid premium rose to several hundred dollars per tonne. For European buyers, the premium became a larger swing factor than the LME itself.

## Why the premium matters more than the index

A European buyer's cost is **LME + premium + conversion**. When smelting capacity leaves a region, the premium captures the replacement cost of importing metal into it. That premium:

- is set by physical availability, not by futures positioning;
- moves slower than the LME but further;
- is regional — a buyer in Italy and a buyer in Turkey pay different numbers for the same metal;
- does not appear in most public price charts that buyers use for negotiation.

## What it means for buyers

- **Track the premium as a separate line.** A quote that folds the premium into "market price" hides the most volatile component.
- **European delivery is now an import decision.** For anything duty-paid into the EU, the question is which non-EU smelter can deliver and under what trade measure.
- **Consider specification flexibility.** Where the standard allows it, moving from a European-produced alloy to an imported equivalent with the same EN/ASTM designation can save the premium, at the cost of longer lead time.
- **Check the carbon line early.** The capacity that left Europe was the highest-cost and often the highest-carbon; CBAM, which entered its transitional phase a year later, priced exactly that gap.

## Where it points

Europe's 2022 closures are the reason the CBAM debate became urgent rather than theoretical: the EU was importing more aluminium and wanted the carbon cost to travel with it. That chain — energy crisis, capacity exit, import dependence, carbon border price — runs directly from 2022 to the 2026 CBAM payment period.
