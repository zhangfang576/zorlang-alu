By mid-August 2026, LME aluminium inventories had fallen by more than half over the year to the lowest level since 1990. On 2 April the exchange held about **416,800 t**, down from roughly **502,000 t** at the start of January — a 16.9% decline in a quarter, and 86% below the 2020 peak of around 3 Mt.

## What low stocks actually mean

Exchange inventory is not total inventory, but it is the only stock that can settle a futures contract. When it falls below the level needed to cover normal delivery demand, three things happen in sequence:

1. **Backwardation becomes persistent.** Cash trades above three-month, because nearby metal is scarcer than future metal.
2. **The cost of borrowing metal rises.** A fabricator needing prompt tonnage pays a premium to whoever holds warranted stock.
3. **Small disruptions become large price moves.** With no buffer, a single smelter outage or shipping delay moves the price more than the event deserves.

In 2026 all three appeared. By August, the domestic Chinese picture was calmer — Changjiang A00 aluminium traded around **RMB 23,680/t** on 23 August, with Guangdong Nanchu around RMB 23,850/t — but the international forward curve stayed tight.

## Why the buffer disappeared

- **Chinese output is capped**, so the metal that used to flow into LME warehouses during surplus periods no longer appears.
- **Just-in-time purchasing became universal.** After three years of volatile prices, fabricators hold minimum inventory and buy against confirmed orders.
- **Financing costs discouraged storage.** Holding metal as collateral stopped being attractive when interest rates and contango both worked against it.
- **Russian metal left the deliverable pool** after the 2024 measures, shrinking the warrantable base even without a change in production.

## What it means for buyers

- **Cover prompt requirements earlier.** In a low-stock market, the lead time that matters is not the mill's rolling schedule but the availability of the metal behind it.
- **Price the backwardation into your contract.** If your reference price is LME cash and your delivery is three months out, you are quoting against the wrong point on the curve.
- **Treat allocation agreements as the primary contract.** For 2027 planning, volume certainty is worth more than a favourable index formula.
- **Expect squeezes to be short and violent.** The 2026 pattern — sharp cash spikes followed by fast reversals — is what a low-inventory market does. Buyers who chase the spike pay twice.

## Where it points

Three decades of aluminium pricing assumed that inventory would absorb shocks. In 2026 it did not. The market has moved to a system where **supply is capped, stocks are thin and policy sets the marginal cost** — a combination that produces both a higher floor and more frequent spikes.
