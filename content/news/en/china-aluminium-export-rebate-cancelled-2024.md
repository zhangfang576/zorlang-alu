On 15 November 2024, China's Ministry of Finance and State Taxation Administration announced the cancellation of the export tax rebate on aluminium and copper products, effective **1 December 2024**. The rebate had been 13%. The products covered accounted for **99.7% of China's aluminium product exports** — about 4.66 Mt in the first nine months of 2024 alone.

## What changed, mechanically

Under the old regime, an exporter of aluminium sheet bought domestic metal including 13% VAT and received that 13% back on export. The effect was to make Chinese export prices roughly 11.5% lower than an equivalent VAT-free price.

Removing the rebate means Chinese aluminium semis now leave the country at **VAT-inclusive cost**. There is no subsidy to absorb. Three consequences followed within weeks:

- **Export prices rose** by roughly the rebate amount for products where the exporter had been passing it through.
- **Margins compressed** for exporters who had been keeping part of the rebate as profit rather than passing it to the buyer.
- **The SHFE–LME relationship inverted.** Exports became less attractive at any given LME/SHFE spread, which tended to support LME and pressure SHFE — the reverse of the pre-2024 relationship.

## The part buyers misread

Many overseas buyers assumed prices would fall because "China needs to export". They did not. What fell was the number of Chinese exporters able to compete on price alone. Smaller rerollers without export channels or with thin margins withdrew, and the export book concentrated in larger mills.

By mid-2025, Chinese aluminium product exports were running below the 2024 pace in volume terms while unit values were higher. **Less tonnage, better price** — the opposite of the pre-2024 pattern.

## What it means for buyers

- **Re-base your China benchmark.** Any price list or cost model built before December 2024 understates Chinese offers by roughly the rebate amount. Comparisons against old quotes are meaningless.
- **Expect conversion margins to be quoted openly.** With the rebate gone, mills quote processing fees more transparently, which makes it easier to see what you are actually paying for.
- **Renegotiate incoterms and VAT treatment.** Confirm whether the price is VAT-exclusive for export, and who bears any future change in rebate policy.
- **Diversify on capability, not only on country.** The exporters that survived are the ones with certification, alloy breadth and stable quality. That is now the relevant shortlist.

## Where it points

The rebate cancellation removed the last structural discount in Chinese aluminium exports. Every price since December 2024 — including the 2025 tariff response and the 2026 CBAM calculation — sits on a higher, cleaner base. For buyers, the practical meaning is that **Chinese aluminium is now priced on cost, not on subsidy**.
