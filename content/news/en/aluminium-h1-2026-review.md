LME aluminium began 2026 around **USD 2,986/t** and traded above **USD 3,500/t** in April — a level last seen in 2022. Unlike the 2021 and 2022 spikes, the first half of 2026 did not end with a round trip. The price centre moved up and held.

## What drove it

**Inventory was already thin.** LME stocks started the year near 500 kt and fell steadily, reaching the lowest level in decades by the second quarter. Thin inventory does not cause a rally, but it removes the buffer that would stop one.

**Chinese supply had no elasticity.** With operating capacity against a 45 Mt ceiling and utilisation near 96%, there was no restartable capacity to answer higher prices.

**Bauxite and alumina were politically priced.** Guinea's export-policy debate added a premium to Chinese smelting costs that had not existed in 2024.

**Demand rotated rather than fell.** Construction remained weak in China, but grid investment, photovoltaic mounting, EV structures and packaging absorbed the difference. Global primary output grew more slowly than demand, and the deficit carried over from 2025.

**Financial positioning followed the fundamentals.** Managed-money length rose to its highest since 2024, which amplified moves in both directions but did not create the trend.

## What was different from 2022

| Dimension | 2022 spike | H1 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Trigger | War and energy shock | Structural deficit and low stocks |
| Inventory | Rebuilt during the spike | Already at multi-decade lows |
| Demand | Falling | Flat to growing |
| Outcome | Full retracement | Higher floor retained |

The table is the whole argument: a price driven by fear reverts, a price driven by a physical deficit does not.

## What it means for buyers

- **Budget on the floor, not the average.** For 2026 procurement, a floor near USD 2,900–3,000/t has held all year. Planning at the 2024 average will produce a budget you cannot execute.
- **Availability beats price.** In a structurally short market, a supplier who can guarantee tonnage through a squeeze is worth more than a 1% cheaper quote.
- **Shorten quote validity again.** The 7-day validity that became normal in 2021 is back; 30-day fixed offers are being priced with a risk premium.
- **Watch the cash–3M spread.** In H1 2026 backwardation appeared repeatedly before each leg up. It is the most reliable short-term signal in a low-inventory market.

## Where it points

The second half of 2026 opened with LME guidance in the USD 3,000–3,500/t range and a market that had learned not to expect Chinese supply to rescue it. For exporters, the operative conclusion is that **2026 pricing is a cost-plus market** — the metal, the carbon and the tariff each have to be quoted explicitly, because none of them is absorbing the others any more.
