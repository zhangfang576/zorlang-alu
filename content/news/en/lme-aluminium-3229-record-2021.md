On 19 October 2021, LME aluminium traded at **USD 3,229/t**, its highest level since 2008. The move capped a twelve-month rally of more than 60% and was driven by three unrelated constraints arriving at the same time.

## The three constraints

**1. European power.** Natural gas prices in Europe rose several-fold into the autumn, making primary smelting uneconomic at any aluminium price below roughly USD 3,000/t. Smelters in Spain, Romania, Slovakia and the Netherlands curtailed output. Europe was exporting its energy problem as an aluminium shortage.

**2. Chinese dual control.** Provincial energy-intensity targets were enforced hard in the third quarter, with production cuts across Inner Mongolia, Guangxi, Xinjiang and Yunnan. Chinese output fell month-on-month for the first time in years.

**3. Logistics and inventories.** LME stocks were falling while off-warrant stocks were being drawn down. Freight was expensive and unreliable, so metal in the wrong place could not arbitrage the difference quickly.

## What broke the rally

The peak did not break on demand. It broke on **coal policy**. In the last week of October, Chinese authorities intervened in thermal coal prices, which collapsed. Since roughly 80% of Chinese aluminium smelting is coal-powered, the input cost fell faster than the metal price, and the market immediately re-priced the marginal cost curve.

By the end of November, LME aluminium was back near **USD 2,600/t** — a 20% round trip in five weeks — while physical premiums in Europe stayed high, because physical tightness and futures positioning are different things.

## What it means for buyers

- **A peak in the futures price is not a peak in your delivered cost.** European duty-paid premiums kept rising for months after the LME turned.
- **Cost-push rallies reverse fast.** When the rally is driven by an input that policy can move (coal, gas, power tariffs), the correction is usually faster and deeper than the climb.
- **Do not chase the top with fixed-price contracts.** In October 2021 buyers who fixed Q1 2022 tonnage at spot paid 15–25% above what was available in January.
- **Watch the spread, not the level.** The cash–3M spread and regional premiums told you where physical metal actually was; the headline told you where paper was.

## Where it points

October 2021 was the dress rehearsal for 2022. It proved that **energy, not ore, sets the marginal cost of aluminium** in Europe and China alike — a relationship that has held through every subsequent shock, including the 2026 squeeze.
