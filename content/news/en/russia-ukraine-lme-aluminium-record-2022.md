On 7 March 2022, LME aluminium closed at **USD 4,073.5/t**, an all-time high. The trigger was the Russian invasion of Ukraine, but the magnitude came from how much of the chain ran through two countries.

## What the market was actually pricing

- **Primary metal.** Russia produced roughly 3.8–4.0 Mt of primary aluminium a year, about 5–6% of global output. Sanctions or self-sanctioning would remove it from Western supply without removing it from the world.
- **Alumina.** Russia and Ukraine together supplied a meaningful share of the world's alumina, including the Nikolaev refinery in Ukraine, which stopped shipping. Alumina is the immediate feedstock for electrolysis; a shortage there tightens metal within weeks.
- **Energy.** The invasion pushed European gas and power prices to records, deepening the smelter curtailments that had started in 2021.

Three supply risks on one metal, arriving in the same fortnight, is what produces a record rather than a rally.

## The reversal

The record did not hold. Within weeks the price fell back toward USD 3,300/t and then below USD 3,000/t by mid-year, as three things happened:

1. Russian metal kept flowing to buyers outside the sanctioning bloc, mostly in Asia.
2. Chinese demand weakened sharply as property construction slowed.
3. The LME chose not to ban Russian metal outright, choosing instead a consultation that ran into 2023.

The record was a **risk premium**, and risk premiums decay unless the risk materialises.

## What it means for buyers

- **Geographic concentration is a price risk, not a compliance risk.** Buyers who had mapped their mill's alumina and power sources saw the exposure before their competitors did.
- **Sanctions create two markets.** From March 2022 onward there was a Western price and a non-Western price for the same metal. If your supply chain touches both, expect documentation questions.
- **Origin became a procurement criterion.** Until 2022, few aluminium purchase contracts specified origin beyond country of manufacture. After 2022, origin declarations became standard.
- **Do not assume the high is the new floor.** Records set on geopolitical fear mean-revert; records set on structural shortage do not. The difference is whether the metal physically moved.

## Where it points

March 2022 set the pattern that later sanctions, the 2024 LME ban and CBAM all followed: **the aluminium price now carries a political layer on top of the cost curve.** For exporters selling into Europe and North America, that layer has to be written into the quote, not absorbed by the margin.
