On 24 February 2023 the United States announced a **200% tariff** on imports of Russian aluminium and aluminium articles, effective 10 March 2023. At that level the duty is not a trade barrier; it is an exclusion. No commercial importer pays 200% and stays in business.

## What happened

- **Russian metal was already marginal in the US.** Volumes had been falling since 2018 sanctions episodes; the 200% duty turned a shrinking trade into a stopped one.
- **The global price barely moved.** The duty shifted trade flows rather than removing tonnage from the world. Russian metal went to Asia and the Middle East; other origins filled the US.
- **The compliance burden grew.** US importers had to evidence origin and, in some cases, the origin of the input aluminium in derivative products.

## The real shift: origin as a commercial term

Before 2022, an aluminium purchase contract specified alloy, temper, dimensions and standard. Origin was a customs field. After this measure, origin became something buyers negotiated on the same page as price — because a change of origin could change the landed duty by more than the metal cost.

Three practical consequences for exporters:

1. **Mill certificates now include origin of primary input.** Buyers ask where the ingot came from, not only where the coil was rolled.
2. **Transit and transformation rules matter.** Rerolling Russian ingot in a third country does not necessarily change origin for duty purposes; substantial transformation tests vary by jurisdiction.
3. **Documentation became a sales capability.** Exporters who could produce a clean origin trail won orders that cheaper competitors lost on paperwork.

## What it means for buyers

- **Ask for origin at RFQ stage.** Discovering an origin problem after the vessel sails is expensive; discovering it before the order is free.
- **Check the derivative list, not just the metal.** Many jurisdictions apply measures to "aluminium articles" and downstream products, so the classification of your part determines the duty.
- **Do not assume a low price means a clean chain.** Post-2023, a quote significantly below market often carries an origin question that has not been resolved.
- **Price the flexibility.** Having a qualified second origin is worth a small premium per tonne; it is worth a great deal the week a measure lands.

## Where it points

The February 2023 action was the first measure that treated aluminium origin as an absolute rather than a tariff variable. It set the template for the 2024 LME delivery ban and for the due-diligence requirements that now sit alongside CBAM reporting.
