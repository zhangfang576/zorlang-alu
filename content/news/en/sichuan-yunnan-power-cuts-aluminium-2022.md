In August 2022, Sichuan ordered industrial users — including all of its aluminium smelters — to suspend production as a record heatwave and drought cut hydropower output and air-conditioning demand peaked. A month later, Yunnan made similar reductions ahead of its dry season. Together they removed a meaningful share of Chinese supply for the second time in eighteen months.

## What happened

- **Sichuan's smelting capacity went to near zero.** The province's roughly 1 Mt of annual capacity was largely idled within days, not weeks.
- **Aluminium was not the priority load.** With reservoirs at multi-decade lows, the province prioritised households. Electrolysis is the most interruptible large load on the grid, and it was interrupted.
- **Yunnan followed in September.** The province asked smelters to reduce load ahead of the dry season, this time pre-emptively rather than in an emergency.
- **The price reacted modestly.** Unlike March 2022, the macro backdrop was deteriorating: Chinese property construction was contracting, and European demand was weakening. LME aluminium traded in a range around USD 2,300–2,500/t rather than spiking.

## The important non-reaction

The 2022 cuts are the most useful case study in the whole cycle, because they show when a supply shock **fails** to move the price. Two conditions were missing:

1. **Inventories were not low enough.** Visible stocks had been rebuilt through the first half of 2022.
2. **Demand was falling faster than supply.** A 2% supply cut does not lift the price when demand is down 5%.

By the fourth quarter, LME aluminium was testing levels below USD 2,200/t despite the outages. The lesson: **supply shocks only move prices when the market is already tight.**

## What it means for buyers

- **Do not buy on news of a production cut.** Buy on the combination of cut plus low inventory plus stable demand. In August 2022 buyers who chased the headline paid 10% above the December price.
- **Seasonal risk is now a contracts issue.** Sichuan and Yunnan are both hydro-dependent and both cut in dry years. Force majeure clauses that exclude "power restrictions" are worth arguing over now, not at the point of failure.
- **Check whether the mill has its own generation.** Smelters with captive coal or diversified power kept running; those on provincial hydro did not.
- **Expect extruders to feel it first.** Billet supply from affected provinces tightened before rolled product did, because billet is consumed locally.

## Where it points

The 2022 drought completed the market's education: Chinese aluminium supply is **policy-managed, weather-exposed and provincial**. The capacity ceiling introduced in 2017 stopped being an abstraction and became an operating constraint with a seasonal rhythm.
