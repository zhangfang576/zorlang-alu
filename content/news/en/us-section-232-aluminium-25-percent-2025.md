On 12 March 2025 the United States restored the Section 232 aluminium tariff to **25%** and removed the country-specific exemptions, quotas and product exclusions that had softened the measure since 2018. The same proclamation extended duties to a longer list of derivative aluminium products.

## What was different this time

The 2018 version of Section 232 was porous. Canada and Mexico had quotas; the EU, Japan and the UK had negotiated arrangements; and thousands of product exclusions had been granted. By 2024 a large share of US aluminium imports entered at effective rates well below 25%.

The 2025 version closed those gaps:

- **No country carve-outs** at the metal level (a separate arrangement for the UK persisted for a period).
- **Derivatives covered.** Extrusions, sheet, strand, cable and a widening list of finished articles were brought into scope, so routing aluminium through a downstream product no longer avoided the duty.
- **No exclusion process** of the earlier kind.

## What it did to the market

- **The US Midwest premium rose** and stayed elevated, because the duty is paid on delivery into the US and is recovered through the premium.
- **Canadian supply re-routed where it could.** Canada is the largest single supplier to the US; where the duty applied, volumes were redirected or absorbed at lower margins.
- **Global prices were largely unaffected.** The US is a premium market, not the marginal buyer. Removing metal from it did not tighten the world.
- **Asian and European exporters re-focused.** The US became a harder market to serve from China, and attention shifted further toward the EU, Middle East, Southeast Asia and Latin America.

## What it means for buyers

- **US landed cost is dominated by the premium, not the LME.** A US buyer's aluminium exposure is roughly LME plus Midwest premium plus duty. Hedging only the LME hedges the smallest moving part.
- **Check whether your part is a "derivative".** The classification determines the duty. Two similar parts can face very different treatment based on how they are described and entered.
- **Consider first-country transformation carefully.** The substantial transformation test for duty purposes is not the same as the origin test for other regulations.
- **Price the market you are actually selling into.** For exporters, the practical decision is whether a destination can absorb the duty at all. For much of the aluminium product range, the answer shifted to non-US markets.

## Where it points

March 2025 re-established the US as a structurally premium, structurally restricted market. Three months later the rate doubled again to 50%, confirming that the direction was escalation rather than negotiation.
