On 3 June 2025 the US administration raised tariffs on imported steel, aluminium and their derivative products from 25% to **50%**, effective 00:01 on 4 June. Imports from the UK stayed at 25% under a separate arrangement. It is the highest US tariff on these metals in modern records.

## The surprising part: the LME did not care

LME aluminium moved little on the announcement. The reason is arithmetic: the duty applies to imports into one market, and the US is a high-premium market at the end of the chain, not the marginal price-setter. A 50% duty changes who sells into the US; it does not change how much aluminium the world produces.

What moved instead:

- **The US Midwest premium** rose to reflect the higher duty, widening the gap between US and world prices.
- **Trade flows rotated further.** Canadian, Mexican and Asian volumes were redirected; some downstream fabricators relocated finishing operations to enter under a different classification.
- **US fabricators absorbed margin pressure**, because the premium is a cost to them and only partly passable to end customers.

## What actually got more expensive

The duty base is the value of the metal, not the finished part. For a product with high conversion content and low metal content — an anodised extrusion, a machined bracket — the effective duty as a share of the total price is lower than 50%. For a commodity coil, it is close to 50%.

That has produced a quiet reshaping of what the US buys abroad:

| Product type | Metal share of value | Effective duty burden |
|---|---|---|
| Commodity sheet and coil | High | Close to the headline rate |
| Extruded profiles, unfinished | Medium | Moderate |
| Machined or assembled parts | Low | Low relative to price |

The practical export opportunity is therefore not in commodity metal but in **further-processed parts where the metal is a small share of value** — and only for exporters who can document origin and classification properly.

## What it means for buyers and exporters

- **Abandon the idea of "the US price".** Post-2025 there is a US premium market and a world market, and they clear at different levels.
- **Re-quote every US-destination item, by classification.** Applying the headline rate to a high-conversion part overstates the impact; applying it nowhere understates it.
- **Do not assume escalation stops here.** The pattern since 2018 has been ratchet, not cycle. Contracts should have a tariff-change clause that assigns the cost to one party.
- **Redirect volume rather than fight the rate.** For most Chinese aluminium exporters, the EU, Middle East, Southeast Asia and Latin America offer better netbacks than a 50% duty market.

## Where it points

The 50% rate completed the transformation of the US into a closed premium market for aluminium. Combined with the 2024 Chinese rebate cancellation and the 2026 CBAM payment period, the three largest markets now each carry a distinct policy cost. Export pricing has become a market-selection exercise before it is a price exercise.
