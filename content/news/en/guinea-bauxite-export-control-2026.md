In late May 2026, Guinea's mining ministry announced that a bauxite export control framework would be finalised in June. On the news, the most-active alumina contract on the Shanghai Futures Exchange rose about **5% intraday, touching roughly RMB 2,860/t**. Guinea produces more than a third of the world's bauxite and shipped about **183 Mt in 2025**, up roughly a quarter year-on-year.

## Why one country's export policy moves the aluminium price

The chain runs: bauxite → alumina → primary aluminium. Roughly two tonnes of bauxite make one tonne of alumina, and roughly two tonnes of alumina make one tonne of aluminium. China imports the majority of its bauxite, and Guinea is its largest source.

That means a Guinean export decision is, within weeks, a Chinese smelting cost decision. The 2026 announcement mattered for three reasons:

- **Volume concentration.** No other country can replace a meaningful share of 183 Mt quickly. Indonesian and Australian supply can grow, but not at that scale in a season.
- **Policy uncertainty, not volume loss.** The announced framework did not specify a cut, but quotas and licensing create uncertainty, and uncertainty is priced immediately in a market running at high utilisation.
- **Alumina was already tight.** With Chinese alumina refineries running hard and bauxite prices having fallen 20–50% from 2025 highs, there was limited buffer.

## The counter-argument

Chinese alumina capacity had been in surplus through 2025 and into 2026, and domestic alumina inventories were not low. Some analysts argued the policy could not tighten short-term supply because refining capacity, not ore, was the binding constraint. That view explains why the alumina rally faded within weeks rather than extending — the announcement moved the price, but the physical balance did not change.

## What it means for buyers

- **Bauxite is the new leading indicator.** For aluminium, watch Guinea shipping data and Chinese bauxite port inventories before watching the metal price.
- **Alumina volatility reaches sheet and foil fastest.** Alumina cost feeds into smelter cost, then into ingot, then into rolled product. The lag is typically four to eight weeks.
- **Do not confuse a policy headline with a supply loss.** In 2026 the announcement moved futures, not cargoes. Confirm with shipment and inventory data before repricing a contract.
- **Origin diversification cuts both ways.** Buyers pressing for non-Chinese metal should note that the alternative supply chains are often more exposed to single-country bauxite risk, not less.

## Where it points

Guinea's export policy is the upstream version of the same story that has run through aluminium since 2021: **policy, not geology, sets supply**. Whether or not the framework reduces tonnage, it has added a permanent political premium to the cost of Chinese aluminium.
